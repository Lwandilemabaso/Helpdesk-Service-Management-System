
# Project Initiation Document

## 1. Document Information

- **Project Name:** Computer Repair Service Management System
- **Project Type:** Business Analyst Portfolio Case Study
- **Document:** Project Initiation Document
- **Status:** In Progress

## 2. Business Background

A computer repair company provides servicing and repair services for computer components. As the business grows, it needs an effective way to manage customer information, repair requests, quotations, service agreements, payments, repair progress, and component collection.

The proposed Computer Repair Service Management System will support these activities and help the business organise its repair service process.

## 3. Business Problem

The company needs a system to manage the repair process consistently, from the initial customer enquiry to the final payment and collection of repaired components.

Without a suitable system, managing customer details, service agreements, repair statuses, quotations, and payment records may become difficult as the business grows.

## 4. Project Purpose

The purpose of this project is to analyse the business requirements and design a proposed Computer Repair Service Management System.

This case study will demonstrate Business Analysis skills through requirements documentation, business process analysis, use cases, UML diagrams, and software development methodology evaluation.

## 5. Project Objectives

The objectives are to:

- Identify the business problem and stakeholder needs.
- Define the system's functional and non-functional requirements.
- Document business rules and process conditions.
- Describe system interactions through use cases and scenarios.
- Model system behaviour and structure using UML diagrams.
- Evaluate software development methodologies and recommend a suitable approach.
- Present the analysis in a clear and professional portfolio repository.

## 6. Project Scope

### 6.1 In Scope

The proposed system analysis will cover:

- Customer identification and eligibility verification.
- Customer information and repair request registration.
- Computer component and service details.
- Quotations and service agreements.
- Customer acceptance, rejection, and cancellation.
- Deposits, final payments, and receipts.
- Repair status updates.
- Customer access to repair progress.
- Component examination and collection.
- User account administration.
- Business requirements and UML system modelling.

### 6.2 Out of Scope

The following are outside the current case study unless the requirements are expanded:

- Building and deploying a fully operational production system.
- Integrating with real banking or payment gateways.
- Purchasing or managing physical computer repair equipment.
- Implementing real customer accounts or storing actual customer data.

## 7. Stakeholders

### Customer
Requests repair services, provides identification and contact information, reviews quotations, accepts or declines service agreements, pays for services, checks repair progress, and collects components.

### Cashier
Verifies customer eligibility, records customer and component details, prepares quotations and service agreements, records payments, issues receipts, and processes cancellations.

### Technician
Carries out repair work and updates the status and outcome of repaired components.

### System Administrator
Manages authorised user accounts, including technician accounts.

### Business Management
Benefits from a more organised repair service process and clearer records of customer requests, repair progress, and payments.

## 8. High-Level Business Rules

- Customers must be over 18 years old to proceed with a repair application.
- Valid identification must be checked before faulty components are added to the repair checklist.
- The cashier records the required customer details.
- The customer must review a quotation before deciding whether to proceed.
- If the customer rejects the quotation, the repair process is cancelled.
- If the customer accepts the quotation, the service agreement must be signed and the required deposit paid before repair work proceeds.
- Technicians update repair statuses.
- Final payment takes place after the servicing is completed and the customer is satisfied.
- Customers collect components at the company's office.
